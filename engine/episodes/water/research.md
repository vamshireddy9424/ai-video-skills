# Hyderabad water crisis × El Niño — research (2026-10-29)

Sources are news reports dated 22–28 Oct 2026 unless noted. Where reports disagree, the figure used in the script is marked ✅.

## 1. El Niño — what it is, how strong, how long
- WMO confirmed El Niño developing on 2 Jun 2026.
- Record strength: Niño 3.4 at +3.3°C in mid-Oct (IRI); Niño-3 at +4.0°C in Sep, beating the Dec 1997 record of +3.6°C (JMA).
- NOAA: >83% chance of a strong to very strong El Niño through Jan–Mar 2027 ✅. WMO: ~100% chance it persists through Feb 2027, peaking around the end of 2026.
- End: one analog outlook (Climate Impact Co., Aug) says it ends abruptly in mid-2027 and may flip to La Niña. This is not official, so present it as "experts anchana" (experts estimate).
- Heat lags the peak, so the biggest warming comes in 2027, which means summer 2027 will be hotter and evaporation higher.
- Mechanism (for the 3D): trade winds weaken, warm Pacific water slides east, the rain-making convection moves away from India, and the monsoon weakens.

## 2. What happened — monsoon 2026
- India: 759 mm, 13% below LPA, the weakest since 2015 (IMD, 30 Sep).
- Telangana: 615.5 mm against 740.6 mm normal, -17% ✅ (Jun 1–Sep 30). A 28% figure appears in earlier, mid-season reports.
- 24 Oct GO: 365 mandals in 31 districts declared drought-hit (Kharif 2026).
- Hyderabad, October: 2.8 mm against 88.5 mm normal ✅. Only 3 of 16 city mandals got adequate rain.
- Kharif sowing about 15 lakh acres against about 29 lakh the year before (end of June). The CM asked farmers to skip one paddy crop ("crop holiday").

## 3. Where Hyderabad's water comes from (HMWSSB)
| Source | River | Normal drawal |
|---|---|---|
| Akkampally (Nagarjuna Sagar) | Krishna | 270 MGD |
| Sripada Yellampally | Godavari | 162 MGD |
| Singur + Manjira | Manjira | ~75 MGD (Singur) |
| Osman Sagar + Himayat Sagar | Musi | small |
- Total normal is about 597 MGD; the official October figure is about 560 MGD ✅. Krishna supplies about half the city.
- Krishna water is pumped about 110 km uphill (the city is roughly 500 m higher than the rivers). Used as a visual; the height is approximate.

## 4. Where the reservoirs are now
- State major and medium: 179.6 of 585 TMC (about 31%).
- Nagarjuna Sagar: about 184–188 TMC of 312, but only about 57 TMC can flow out by gravity. The minimum draw-down level is 510 ft, and about 131 TMC sits below it. HMWSSB has tendered **emergency pumps**.
- Srisailam: about 27%, having lost about 101 TMC between 8 Aug and 13 Oct.
- Singur: about 3–3.9 of 29.9 TMC (about 10%) ✅. Newsmeter: "only 50 days of water in Singur".
- Osman Sagar: 3.5 → 2.2 TMC. Himayat Sagar: 2.5 → 2.1 TMC.
- HMWSSB: some reservoirs may run out of usable water between **mid-December and the end of December**.

## 5. What it looks like in the city now
- Supply moved from alternate days to **once every 3 days** for major parts of the city (HMWSSB MD Ashok Reddy). Secunderabad Cantonment and the outskirts get water once every 3–4 days.
- Tankers: **6,000–8,000 bookings a day**, against a normal off-season of 800–1,500. October bookings: 62,565, up 233% from 18,806.
- Supply gap: about 585 MGD supplied against about 735 MGD needed, a **150 MGD** gap. Owaisi separately flags a 65 MGD shortfall (different basis).
- Borewells: 14,000+ dry this year (as of Jul). Apartment borewells failing went from about 4,000 to about 12,000. Wells deeper than 1,000 ft are failing.
- Groundwater statewide: average depth 5.78 → 7.87 m below ground (more than 2 m deeper).
- About 3 lakh connections could face shortages by December.
- Water mafia: tankers wearing fake HMWSSB stickers (HYDRAA bust). Residents of Shivarampally and Rajendranagar allege over-extraction; these are allegations.
- Waste: Greater Hyderabad uses about 2,600 million litres a day, and "nearly half" is reportedly lost (political claim; mark as reported).

## 6. Future — if El Niño continues
- CM Revanth Reddy has warned of a water and **power** crisis lasting **8–10 months, until July–August 2027**. All reservoir water is reserved for drinking, with a reassessment in December.
- Timeline for the script:
  - **Nov–Dec 2026:** El Niño peaks. Rain is near zero, since the northeast monsoon is also weak in El Niño years (in 2015 it was 76% short). Some reservoirs hit dead storage in December.
  - **Jan–Mar 2027:** El Niño is still strong. Emergency pumping starts from below Sagar's minimum draw-down level. Tanker demand rises further.
  - **Apr–Jun 2027:** Summer, and hotter than usual because the heat lags the El Niño peak. Evaporation peaks, demand peaks (835 MGD projected for 2027) and borewells fail. This is the danger window.
  - **Jun–Jul 2027:** The first real relief can only come from the 2027 monsoon. If El Niño fades by mid-2027 or La Niña follows, the monsoon could recover. That is not guaranteed, and IMD's 2027 forecast only comes in April.
- 2015–16 precedent: southwest monsoon -15%, northeast monsoon -76%, groundwater down 2.78 m, ₹791 crore NDRF relief.
- Long term: demand is 835 MGD in 2027 and about 1,114 MGD by 2047. Godavari Phase II/III (₹7,360 crore, 20 TMC, of which 16 TMC is drinking water and 4 TMC for lakes and the Musi) aims to add about 300 MGD by 2030.
- Lakes: about 61% of lake area lost between 1979 and 2024 (NRSC, via a secondary source, so treat with caution). HYDRAA has reclaimed 1,350 acres since July 2024.

## 7. Solutions / what people can do
- HMWSSB "Catch the Rain": converting dead borewells into recharge wells, and using treated wastewater for construction, industry and gardens.
- Rainwater-harvesting pits, fixing leaks, restoring lakes.
